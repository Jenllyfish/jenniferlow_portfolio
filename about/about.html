HEY! I'M JEN!

Jennifer “Jen” Low is a digital illustrator and multimedia artist who uses surreal imagery to convey existential themes underlying everyday life. Having grown up in the heart of California’s Central Valley, she currently resides in the Bay Area, studying Digital Media Art at San Jose State University. Currently, Jen is involved with CADRE.SO as the organization’s social media manager, subsequenting her involvement with their “Collective Bloom” project. Utilizing a plethora of digital software, she typically illustrates with Photoshop and designs in Illustrator, before importing her 2D assets into After Effects for animation. Within the realm of 3D artwork, Jen models singular props or lively environments in Blender. Often inspired by vintage aesthetics and obsolete technology, Jen incorporates these motifs to convey a sense of nostalgia for an optimistic future. Whether a simple sketch or rendered illustration, Jen’s work rarely holds little meaning to her: each of her creative endeavors stemming from a need to express her unconventional inner world.

ARTIST STATEMENT

“The world as you know it is dead and gone, and there’s nothing you can do about it...”

This is the notion that has haunted me throughout my life, and the manifesto I use to propel my creative drive.

Growing up I quickly learned that I would be different from others in my social circle and had to understand that stability is a fleeting luxury. If I wanted a sense of normalcy, I would have to find that whilst acknowledging that the societal definition of “normal” would not apply to me. Nevertheless, I consider my artwork an extension of my inner world, and an escape from the pressures that have been forced upon me since I began to live. Vintage aesthetics have always been a fascination of mine, inspired by my childhood connection to media from the mid-to-late 20th century. With the 2020s resurgence of analog media, and my fond memories of nighttime cartoons on my family’s old CRT television, I felt called to utilize the iconic grain of obsolete technology to recreate the nostalgic “hope for the future” from my childhood. Aside from this however, I aim to question what our legacy as humans is on our home planet, with examples of our past in comparison to our current societal attitude toward the environment.

The world as you know it is dead and gone, and there’s nothing you can do about it …except to create a better one in its place.
